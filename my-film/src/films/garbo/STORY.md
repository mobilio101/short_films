# GARBO: Short plan (draft, not animated yet)

- Format: YouTube Short, 1080×1920, 60 seconds
- Language and voice: English, ElevenLabs Brian (`eleven_v3`, emotion tags)
- Style: Newsprint, with the full acting range (EMOTE poses, faces and body movement)
- Title options: "The Spy Who Invented 27 Spies" (first choice), "He Fooled Hitler With Fake Friends",
  "The Double Agent Both Sides Gave a Medal"
- End card: **AGENT GARBO.**

## Facts (checked online)

| Fact | Source |
|---|---|
| Juan Pujol García, a Spaniard who had run a poultry farm, offered to spy for Britain and was turned down. | Wikipedia, We Are The Mighty, National WWII Museum |
| He then got himself hired by German intelligence (the Abwehr) to spy in Britain, but went to Lisbon instead and invented his reports from a tourist guide to Britain, train timetables, reference books, magazines and newsreels. | Wikipedia, Liberation Route Europe |
| He invented a network of fictional sub-agents, 27 by 1944, which the Germans funded. When one "died", the Germans sent money for his "widow". | MI5, agentgarbo.com, Espionage History |
| British codebreakers intercepted his reports; they were convincing enough that MI5 hunted for the spy ring. Britain then recruited him (MI5 codename GARBO). | Wikipedia, MI5 |
| Operation Fortitude: on 9 June 1944 he told the Germans that Normandy was a diversion and the main invasion would hit the Pas-de-Calais. The message reached Hitler, and Germany kept major forces waiting at the Pas-de-Calais through July and August. | MI5, The National Archives, History.com |
| He received the Iron Cross from Germany and an MBE from Britain. | MI5, Wikipedia |
| (Not used, but true and good for a sequel: he faked his death in Angola in 1949 and was found in Venezuela in 1984.) | History.com, agentgarbo.com |

Dramatic licence (agreed approach): "They laugh him out the door" (on record he was simply turned
down), "Pujol tells Hitler" (the message was passed on to Hitler), and the comic acting. Left out: the
popular "Glasgow litre of wine" anecdote, which could not be confirmed.

## Voiceover script (about 150 words, emotional roller coaster)

One man fooled Hitler with twenty-seven spies who never existed.
Nineteen forty. Juan Pujol, a Spanish chicken farmer, hates the Nazis. He begs the British to let him
spy for them. They laugh him out the door.
So he does something insane. He goes to the Nazis instead. They hire him to spy on Britain.
But Pujol never goes. He sits in Lisbon with a guidebook and train timetables, inventing everything.
Fake reports. Fake spies. Twenty-seven of them. The Germans pay them all.
His lies are so good, British intelligence starts hunting for his spy ring. Then they find him, and
hire him. Now he lies for Britain.
D-Day. Pujol tells Hitler: Normandy is a trick. The real attack is coming at Calais.
They believe him. Their tanks wait for an army that never comes.
His reward? An Iron Cross from Germany. A medal from Britain.
Twenty-seven spies. Not one of them was real.

Emotional curve: hook (impossible claim) → hope and humiliation (laughed out) → reckless courage
(goes to the Nazis) → comic tension (the guidebook spy, fake spies multiplying) → danger and twist
(British hunt him, then hire him) → climax (D-Day, the big lie) → triumph (tanks waiting for nothing) →
absurd payoff (medals from both sides) → loop line back to the hook.

## Cast and acting

- **Pujol** (red, thin moustache, flat cap): eager → crushed → reckless grin → sweaty, scribbling
  genius → smug → nervous → triumphant.
- **British officers** (white, peaked caps): laughing, then shocked, then shaking his hand.
- **German handlers** (beige, peaked caps, no Nazi symbols): stern, then delighted, counting money,
  then nodding at the map.
- **Fake spies**: identical little beige figures that pop into existence one after another.
