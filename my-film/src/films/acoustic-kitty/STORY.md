## Script (about 170 words, about 52 s at Brian's pace)
What happens if the CIA turns a cat into a spy? In the nineteen sixties, they found out.
The target was the Soviets. The problem was getting close. But a cat can go anywhere, and nobody
looks twice. So surgeons hid a microphone in its ear, a transmitter at the base of its skull, and an
antenna in its fur. Years of work. Millions of dollars.
Then came the first mission. A park in Washington, outside a Soviet compound. Two men on a bench. The
cat had one job: listen.
But the cat didn't care.
You see, the CIA could train it to walk short distances. It could not make it want to. A cat does
what a cat wants.
So the agents wrote their final report. Cats can be trained to move a short way, it said. But in a
real foreign operation, quote, "it would not be practical."
In nineteen sixty-seven, the project was cancelled.
The CIA built a spy out of a cat. It just forgot the cat had to agree.

(Count the words and time them with the voice before building. Target 165–185.)

*Structure check:* question hook in the first sentence → "But / Then" escalation with the "cat doesn't
care" turn → "You see…" explanation at about 60–70% → "So…" payoff (the memo) → one plain closing line.
No intro, no call to action.

## Full-page fact-check (17 Nov 2026, after en.wikipedia.org and www.history.com were allowed)
Read in full: Wikipedia "Acoustic Kitty" and History.com "When the CIA Learned Cats Make Bad Spies"
(Becky Little, updated 28 May 2025).
- 1960s CIA project (Directorate of Science & Technology), aimed at the Kremlin and Soviet embassies: OK.
- Microphone in the ear canal, transmitter at the base of the skull, wire/antenna in the fur: OK.
- About $20 million (Victor Marchetti): OK, the script only says "millions".
- First mission: two men on a bench in a park outside the Soviet embassy in Washington (Wikipedia);
  History.com says two men sitting on a bench in the park: OK ("Soviet compound" = the embassy grounds).
- Trainable for short distances; the closing memo quote "the environmental and security factors ... it
  would not be practical" is word for word in Wikipedia (CIA memo "Views on Trained Cats Use", March 1967): OK.
- Cancelled 1967, disclosed 2001: OK.
- **One small wording issue:** both sources say *a* (veterinary) surgeon did the operation; the voice says
  "surgeons". Reported to the user; not changed (changing it means re-recording the voice).
- Both pages tell the taxi story; the film still says and shows nothing about the cat's fate.
