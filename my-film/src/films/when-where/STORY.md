## Facts and sources
Study: Milne, Orbell & Sheeran (2002), "Combining motivational and volitional interventions to promote
exercise participation: Protection motivation theory and implementation intentions", *British Journal of
Health Psychology* 7(2):163-184, https://doi.org/10.1348/135910702169420

What the film says, and where it comes from:
1. People who were only motivated (told why exercise matters) wanted to exercise more, but did not
   actually exercise significantly more. (Abstract: the motivational intervention raised threat and
   coping appraisal and intentions, but did not significantly increase later exercise.)
2. People who got the same motivation **plus** a plan of when and where they would exercise did
   exercise more. (Abstract: adding implementation intentions had a significant effect on exercise;
   later reviews call it "dramatic".) The plan was written down by the participants (when and where
   in the coming week): from the backlog note and the NCI summary page; to be re-checked in the full text.
3. The plan did not make people *want* it more; it changed what they *did*. (Abstract: the volitional
   part did not change intention or other motivation measures.)

**No numbers in the film.** The full paper and the summary pages could not be opened from this
cloud session (doi.org, repository.essex.ac.uk, search.pedro.org.au and cancercontrol.cancer.gov are
blocked by the network settings), so the percentages often quoted online were not checked in the
original and are left out, as agreed. The narration says "뚜렷하게" (clearly), not "몇 배".
Where the facts came from this time: a web search summary of the abstract and of later reviews
(sources listed there: repository.essex.ac.uk/12693, PEDro record 10718, Prestwich et al. 2015 review).
If the user allows `repository.essex.ac.uk` or `doi.org`, I re-check points 1 to 3 in the full text
before recording.

Other sources:
- https://cancercontrol.cancer.gov/brp/research/constructs/implementation-intentions (NCI, what an
  implementation intention is: "when situation X arises, I will do Y")
- https://repository.essex.ac.uk/12693/ (University of Essex repository record of the paper)

Safety: exercise advice stays general and gentle (a walk in the park), no health claims, no numbers.

## Scenes (our own scenes for this film; only the look, characters and split-screen idea come from the test)
Setting: a small studio flat at night. Light sources: the phone glow, the yellow desk lamp, a
streetlight outside the window, later the park streetlight. Top panel 게으른 나, bottom panel 한걸음 나.

1. **Intro, 0–2 s, three cuts under 1 s each, camera pushing in** (under line 1): running shoes by the
   front door, still with the shop tag; a phone lighting up with the reminder "이번 주 운동!" being
   swiped away; the hero's face on the couch, eyebrows sliding down.
2. **Split screen: the resolution** (line 2): the hero on the couch, fist up, eyebrows high. Top panel:
   the shadow appears behind the couch, medium size, and says "그래, 나중에." with a pat on the shoulder.
3. **The excuses grow** (line 3): a wall calendar with no marks; each day the hero looks at the shoes
   and the shadow answers, growing taller each time ("내일 하자…", "주말에 몰아서."). The word "나중에"
   floats in violet, getting bigger.
4. **The lowest point** (line 4): the calendar pages flip in a quick montage (a week passes); the shoes
   gather a thin line of dust by the door; the shadow now fills most of the top panel and lies on the
   couch beside the hero. "다음 주부터." Camera slowly pushes in on the shoes.
5. **The study** (line 5): the split screen becomes two simple groups of little amber figures under one
   lamp (no charts, no numbers): a speech bubble icon with a heart (they hear why exercise is good) and
   their hearts glow bigger, but they stay seated. A thin violet shadow sits behind each.
6. **The difference** (line 6): a second group: same glowing hearts, and each one holds a small paper
   note; a pencil scribbles. Their notes light up warm.
7. **They move** (line 7): the second group's figures stand up and walk off to the right one by one;
   the first group stays. The shadow in the top panel squints, "나중에…?", unsure.
8. **The one step** (line 8): back in the flat, close-up at the desk lamp: the hero writes on a yellow
   sticky note "화 7시, 공원" and sticks it on the front door above the shoes. In the top panel the
   shadow reaches for his word "나중에": the letters crumble and he shrinks with each syllable.
9. **Tuesday, 7 pm** (line 9): the clock shows 7:00, the note glows; the hero puts on the shoes (the tag
   snaps off) and steps outside; the bottom panel grows to fill the screen: an evening park path under
   one streetlight, the hero walking into the light; a tiny shadow trails behind, no bigger than a
   pebble.
10. **End card** (last 2–3 s, under the end of line 9): "언제, 어디서, 정해." in white with the violet
    underline drawing in, over the park light. No fade to black; the loop returns to the shoes by the door.

New props for this film: running shoes with tag, front door, wall calendar, sticky note, desk lamp,
park path and streetlight, the small "study" figures.
