Disagreements, and how the script handles them: the exact Rhine geography (Wesel vs Rheinberg) differs
between sources, so the script only says "crossed somewhere else". Troop numbers for the faked divisions
vary, so the script says "two divisions" and "thousands of men", not an exact figure. The estimate of
15,000 to 30,000 lives saved is left out because it is only an estimate.

## Voiceover script (152 words)

This army fought with balloons. In March, nineteen forty-five, two American divisions appeared on the
banks of the Rhine. Tanks. Trucks. Thousands of men. The Germans moved their defenses to meet them.
There was just one problem. None of it was real. The tanks were rubber, inflated in minutes. The roar of
engines came from speakers on trucks. The radio chatter was scripted. Even the shoulder patches were
fake. This was the Ghost Army: about eleven hundred artists, designers and sound engineers, trained to
fight with paint, noise and imagination. From Normandy to the Rhine, they staged more than twenty
deceptions. At the Rhine, six hundred inflatable vehicles drew the enemy's eyes, while the real
divisions crossed somewhere else. Their secret stayed locked away until nineteen ninety-six. In twenty
twenty-four, they finally received the Congressional Gold Medal. Because sometimes, the most powerful
army on the battlefield is the one that isn't there.

Voice notes: Brian v3 spoke our sample at about 2.1 words per second, which would make this about
70 seconds. When the voice is generated, the speed goes up a little and the emotion tags are kept
light. If it still runs over 60 seconds, cut "trained to fight with paint, noise and imagination" first.
