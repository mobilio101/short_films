## Facts (checked online; sources are search summaries, original pages were blocked)

| Fact | Source |
|---|---|
| Operation Mincemeat was a British deception in 1943, part of the wider Operation Barclay, to make Germany think the Allies would strike Greece and Sardinia, not Sicily. Devised by Charles Cholmondeley and Ewen Montagu. | Wikipedia; History.co.uk; National WWII Museum |
| A body was given the false identity of Royal Marines Major William Martin. The man was Glyndwr Michael, a homeless Welshman who died in London in January 1943. | Wikipedia; History.co.uk; the-past.com |
| Pocket items: ID card, photo of a fiancée ("Pam"), love letters, theatre ticket stubs, keys, a receipt for an engagement ring, tailor and jeweller bills, a Lloyds Bank overdraft demand (£79 19s 2d). The "Pam" photo was of an MI5 staff member; the letters were written by office staff. | Britannica; Secret Scotland; Military.com; Wikipedia (Montagu) |
| HMS Seraph released the body off Huelva, Spain. A fisherman found it; the body reached the Spanish authorities on 30 April 1943. A pro-German Abwehr agent worked in Huelva, which is why the place was chosen. | History.co.uk; The Past; History Collection |
| Spain shared copies of the papers with German intelligence before returning the originals. Hitler was convinced they were genuine (Admiral Canaris of the Abwehr was doubtful). | Wikipedia; History Collection |
| German reinforcements went to Greece and Sardinia; Sicily received none. The Allies landed in Sicily on 9 July 1943 (Operation Husky; some sources say 10 July, so the script says only "July"). | Britannica; Wikipedia; National WWII Museum |
| Historian Michael Howard: perhaps the most successful single deception of the war. Full effect is hard to measure; Sicily fell faster and with fewer losses than predicted. | Wikipedia; National WWII Museum |
| The man's identity stayed secret until Ben Macintyre's research in 1996; a headstone line naming Glyndwr Michael was added in Huelva in 1997 or 1998 (sources differ). | Wikipedia; History.co.uk |

**Not used because it could not be confirmed:** Seraph's exact release date, Churchill's telegram wording,
the chained briefcase, the fisherman's name, the exact cause of death (sources differ: rat poison or
liver failure; the script says only "died in London"). "Alone" or "unclaimed" is not used for the same
reason.

Dramatic licence (the user's rule: core facts true): "So spies invent a man" (a planned invention),
"Hitler believes it" (he accepted the papers as real), the comic and sad acting.

## Voiceover script (155 words)

Who tricked Hitler, and never even knew it?
April 1943. Britain needs to hide an invasion. So spies invent a man. Major William Martin.
They give him a fiancée named Pam. Her photo. Love letters. A theatre ticket. Even an overdraft. Then
they tuck secret letters in his briefcase and slip him into the sea off Spain.
There's just one problem. Major Martin is dead.
A fisherman pulls him out. Spain hands the papers to the Germans. They read it: the Allies are coming
for Greece. For Sardinia. Not Sicily.
Hitler believes it. His army moves away.
July. The Allies land in Sicily, and almost nobody is waiting.
So who was Major Martin? For more than fifty years, nobody knew. His real name was Glyndwr Michael. A
homeless Welshman who died in London.
He never fought. He never knew. Yet he helped win Sicily.
Hitler trusted a man who never lived. So who tricked Hitler?

Emotional curve: hook (question) → curiosity (the invented man) → warmth (Pam, the love letters) →
dread (he is dead) → tension (the fisherman, the papers) → triumph (Hitler believes it, army moves) →
shock-then-sad (the real name) → bittersweet payoff → loop back to the question.
The last line returns to the hook, so the Short replays.

## Cast and acting

- **Glyndwr Michael** (the main character, red): in the first half he is only a **white silhouette
  with a question mark**, "Major Martin" drawn as a paper cut-out with a stiff pose; from "his real
  name" he becomes a real character in red: tired, gentle, small.
- **Montagu and the British spies** (white, peaked caps, Union Jack chest patch): sly grins, whispers.
- **Pam**: a flat photo on a paper card, a small drawn smile. She is a prop, never a character.
- **Fisherman** (beige-grey): shrugs, stares, runs to deliver the find.
- **German officers** (beige, Balkenkreuz patch, no swastikas): read the papers, then grin, then push
  pins on the map. Hitler is never drawn; only "the order" appears as a stamped paper.
- **InkDrop mascot**: small in the hook, reacting (what? who?), cheers on the end card.
- The shadow is not used.
