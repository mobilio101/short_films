## Facts (checked online)

| Fact | Source |
|---|---|
| Apollo 13 (crew Jim Lovell, Jack Swigert, Fred Haise) was on its way to the Moon when an oxygen tank in the service module exploded on 13 April 1970, about 56 hours into the flight, roughly 200,000 miles from Earth. | NASA, Britannica, History.com |
| "Houston, we've had a problem" (Swigert's words, repeated by Lovell; exact wording varies slightly between sources). The landing was abandoned. | NASA, Space.com |
| The crew moved into the lunar module Aquarius and used it as a lifeboat. Its CO2 filters were enough for two men for about two days; the trip home took about four days with three men. | NASA, Universe Today (Jerry Woodfill) |
| Carbon dioxide built up. The command module's spare lithium hydroxide canisters were square and did not fit the lunar module's round sockets. | NASA SP-350, NASA "Successful Failure" |
| Engineers on the ground (led by Ed Smylie) designed the "mailbox" adapter from items on board: duct tape, plastic bags, the cover of the flight plan and suit hoses, and read the crew the steps. | NASA, Engineering.com, Space.com |
| The powered-down spacecraft was cold (cabin readings around 38°F after contact). | NASA Apollo Flight Journal, Smithsonian |
| Re-entry radio blackout was expected to last about four minutes and lasted about a minute and a half longer. Splashdown on 17 April 1970 near USS Iwo Jima; all three survived. | NASA, UPI, Britannica |
| NASA calls the mission "a successful failure". | NASA |

Dramatic licence: engineers "pile everything onto a table" (they worked from a list of what was on
board), the moment-by-moment acting. Real photo: NASA's own picture of the finished "mailbox" in the
lunar module (AS13-62-8929, public domain as a NASA image; credit NASA).

## Voiceover script (146 words)

Two hundred thousand miles from Earth, Apollo 13 explodes.
April nineteen seventy. Three astronauts are on their way to the Moon. Then, bang. Houston, we've had
a problem. Oxygen is venting into space. The Moon landing is over. Now it's about survival.
They crawl into the lunar lander, a lifeboat built for two men for two days. It has to keep three men
alive for four.
Then a new killer. Carbon dioxide. Every breath makes the air more poisonous. The spare filters are
square. The holes are round.
On Earth, engineers pile everything the astronauts have onto a table. Duct tape. Plastic bags. The
cover of the flight plan. They build a fix, and talk the crew through it, step by step.
It works.
Freezing and exhausted, they head home. Re-entry. Radio silence. Four minutes. Five.
Then a voice. Splashdown.
NASA calls it a successful failure.

Emotional curve: hook (explosion) → excitement (going to the Moon) → shock (bang, the problem) →
dread (oxygen venting, landing over) → claustrophobia (cramped lifeboat) → panic (poisoned air, square
vs round) → frantic hope (engineers improvise) → relief (it works) → agonising silence (blackout) →
explosion of joy (splashdown) → ironic payoff.

## Cast and acting

- **Jim Lovell** (red, the main character), **Swigert** and **Haise** (white): all three in white
  space caps with a small US flag patch.
- **Mission Control** (beige, headsets) and **engineers** (white shirts, ties, glasses).
- Key acting: excited thumbs-up → shock and recoil at the bang → wide-eyed fear as gas vents →
  despair in the cramped lander → coughing, sweating panic as CO2 rises → engineers frantic, sleeves
  rolled up, then triumphant → astronauts shivering, breath clouds → everyone frozen during the
  blackout → Mission Control explodes in cheers, astronauts hug.
