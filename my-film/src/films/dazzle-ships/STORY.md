## Script (181 words, about 55 s at Brian's pace)
In nineteen seventeen, Britain painted its warships to look like zebras. On purpose.
German U-boats were sinking British ships faster than Britain could build them. And you can't hide a
ship on the open sea. So a painter named Norman Wilkinson had a ridiculous idea. If you can't hide it,
confuse it. Black stripes. White zigzags. Blue swirls. The Navy thought he'd lost his mind.
So Wilkinson painted a tiny model ship and set it in front of King George the Fifth. The king looked
through a periscope and called the ship's heading. South. It was pointing east.
You see, a U-boat captain got just a few seconds at the periscope. He had to guess a ship's speed and
direction, then fire where it was going to be. Get it wrong by ten degrees, and the torpedo misses.
So Britain painted thousands of ships. Did it work? Even the Navy's own experts couldn't prove it.
But sailors loved it, and it cost almost nothing.
The paint job that made ships impossible to miss was designed to make them impossible to hit.

*Structure check:* hook in the first sentence (an absurd fact) → "So / But" escalation with the Navy
doubting → the king fooled → "You see…" explanation at about 60–70% → "So…" payoff → one closing
line that loops back to the zebra-striped ships of the hook. No intro, no call to action.

## Cast (Toon characters)
- **Main character as Norman Wilkinson:** round head, round shades, closed smirk (always). Outfit: a
  navy officer's cap and a white painter's smock with paint splashes; holds a paintbrush.
- **Navy brass:** square heads, peaked caps, blue jackets, deadpan, then laughing at him.
- **King George V:** square head, small crown, a neat beard, red coat with gold buttons.
- **U-boat captain:** inverted-triangle head, a plain dark cap (no German insignia), squinting at the
  periscope.
- **Sailors:** round and square heads in striped shirts, cheering.
