## Ideas
- **The Cat Spy ("Acoustic Kitty")**: the CIA surgically wired a cat with a mic and radio to bug the Soviets; first field test, it walked into the street and a taxi hit it. Strange object + absurd plan + the reason it failed (cats do what they want). Smirkman as a deadpan CIA agent; square-head bosses, schemer scientist. Fact check: the taxi detail comes from one researcher's account (Richelson), cost figures vary ($15–20M), documents are partly redacted; say "reportedly" where needed. Sources: https://www.history.com/articles/cia-spy-cat-espionage-fail; https://www.mentalfloss.com/animals/cats/cia-plan-use-cats-spies-and-taxi-ruined-it; https://www.popsci.com/science/article/2013-05/cias-cyborg-cat/; https://boundarystones.weta.org/2023/07/26/federal-governments-15-million-cat
- **The Pigeon Missile**: In WW2 a famous psychologist trained pigeons to peck at a target screen to steer a bomb (three birds, majority vote). It worked in tests, never flew; electronics won. Concrete object + "pointless" paradox + the real reason (it was never silly, just too odd for generals). Fact check: cancelled Oct 1944, revived by the Navy 1948 as Orcon, ended 1953; $25,000 funding. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Pigeon; https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smithsonian-institution/bf-skinners-pigeon-guided-rocket-53443995/; https://taskandpurpose.com/history/project-pigeon-missiles-ww2/
- **The Man Who Sold the Eiffel Tower (twice)**: In 1925 a con man posed as a government official and sold Paris's tower for scrap to a dealer, then came back six months later and did it again. Inverted-triangle schemer vs. a round-head victim; the real reason it worked (the tower was unpopular and meant to be temporary). Fact check: sources disagree on the second sale (dealer went to police vs. too ashamed) and number of dealers; use only what agrees. Sources: https://www.mentalfloss.com/crime/smooth-operator-how-victor-lustig-sold-eiffel-tower; https://www.ripleys.com/stories/sold-eiffel-tower; https://thehustle.co/originals/the-con-man-who-sold-the-eiffel-tower-twice-1; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Lustig
- **The Town Where You Can't Die (Longyearbyen)**: "Dying is illegal here. Yet…" Frozen ground keeps bodies from decaying, so burials stopped around 1950 and the sick are flown out; the 1918 flu victims' preserved bodies even gave scientists live-virus samples. Fact check IMPORTANT: it is not a real written law, only a burial ban and practice; say "you can't be buried here", never "illegal" as a fact. Dates vary between sources (1917/1918 deaths, 1990s/1998 exhumation). Sources: https://theculturetrip.com/europe/norway/articles/why-its-illegal-to-die-in-this-town-in-norway; https://www.outlooktraveller.com/destinations/international/welcome-to-the-town-where-its-illegal-to-die-inside-the-arctic-longyearbyen; https://polarpedia.eu/?p=9872
- **The Penguin Post Office (Port Lockroy)**: A job where you run a post office and gift shop in Antarctica, sort around 80,000 postcards, and count penguins; no running water or flushing toilet. Weakest paradox of the five (needs a stronger "real reason", e.g. the base's WW2 secret-mission past, which I have not yet verified). Sources: https://www.foxweather.com/lifestyle/port-lockroy-post-office-antarctica-base-job; https://www.greenmatters.com/pn/worlds-most-remote-post-office-in-antarctica-has-a-job-that-includes-counting-penguins
