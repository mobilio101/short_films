Concrete object + "pointless" paradox + the real reason (it was never silly, just too odd for generals). YouTube check: only small explainers (Thoughty2 ~2 min, BBC, Skinner Foundation film). Fact check: cancelled Oct 1944, revived by the Navy 1948 as Orcon, ended 1953; $25,000 funding. - **Why the Sea Ends at 3 Miles (the cannon-shot rule)**: "What happens if you sail up to someone's coast and stop where their cannons can't reach?" For about 300 years a country owned only the water its cannons could hit; the law of the sea was literally measured in cannon shots (the three-mile limit). Strange measuring stick + paradox (a border you can't see) + real reason; Smirkman as a captain at the edge of a coast, square-head port officials. Fact check IMPORTANT: Bynkershoek (1702) is credited with the cannon-range idea, the exact 3-mile figure is credited to Galiani, and some historians say it came from the sea 'league', not real cannon range; use only what the sources agree on. Modern limit is 12 nautical miles (UN 1982). YouTube check: no video found in one search, check again before the user commits. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three-mile_limit; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornelius_van_Bynkershoek; https://library.law.yale.edu/news/freedom-seas-part-8; https://historynet.com/where-do-international-waters-begin/
- **The Window Tax (bricked-up windows)**: "What would you do if England taxed your windows?" From 1696 to 1851 houses were taxed by window count, so owners bricked windows shut; the paradox is paying to live in the dark. Concrete object (a bricked-up window) + real reason (hearths were hard to count). Fact check IMPORTANT: the saying 'daylight robbery' is NOT proven to come from it, and not every blocked window was a tax dodge (blind windows were also decoration); do not say either. Repeal 1851. YouTube check: at least one video on it exists (small channel); a classic fact, so we need a fresh angle (e.g. the tax collector arrives at the house). Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Window_tax; https://www.lincolninst.edu/publications/articles/window-tax; https://www.mentalfloss.com/culture/architecture/bricked-up-windows-british-homes
