## Ideas
- **The Corpse That Fooled Hitler** (Operation Mincemeat, 1943): British spies dressed a dead man as a fake Royal Marines major, floated him ashore in Spain with fake invasion plans, and Hitler believed it. Fits: strong hook, clear hero (Montagu, "Major Martin"), twist ending (the real man was a homeless Welshman, Glyndwr Michael, named only in 1996). Sources differ on his cause of death; say "died in London" only. Sources: https://www.nationalww2museum.org/war/articles/secret-agents-secret-armies-operation-mincemeat ; https://the-past.com/feature/the-corpse-that-fooled-hitler/ ; https://www.history.co.uk/article/operation-mincemeat
- **12 Ships vs 133** (Battle of Myeongnyang, Korea, Oct 26, 1597): Admiral Yi Sun-sin faces a huge Japanese fleet with almost no ships. Fits: underdog, tide as the trick, Korean pride. Check first: fleet count is 12 or 13 (most say 12); Japanese losses vary a lot (31 vs about 200 vs disputed), so do not quote a loss number unless a scholarly source is found. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Myeongnyang ; https://www.encyclopedia.com/history/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/yi-sunsin
- **The Town That Couldn't Stop Dancing** (Strasbourg, July 1518): a woman starts dancing in the street and will not stop; within weeks dozens, maybe hundreds, join. Fits: odd, visual, perfect for the counter (DAY 1, DAY 10). Check first: she is usually named Frau Troffea, but counts range 50 to 400 and the cause is unsettled (mass psychogenic illness is the leading theory); tell it as "no one knows why". Sources: https://publicdomainreview.org/essay/the-dancing-plague-of-1518/ ; https://www.nationalgeographic.com/history/article/dancing-plague-of-1518-strasbourg-choreomania ; https://www.history.com/articles/what-was-the-dancing-plague-of-1518
- **The Pigeon That Wouldn't Quit** (Cher Ami, Oct 1918): a wounded carrier pigeon carries the message that tells artillery to stop shelling the "Lost Battalion" (554 men, 77th Division). Fits: cute animal hero, ticking clock, and the mascot can react. Check first: the Smithsonian says only that he died in June 1919 of wounds; details like lost leg and blindness and the exact credit for the rescue are disputed, so say "reportedly". Real photo of the bird: Smithsonian (check licence). Sources: https://prologue.blogs.archives.gov/2017/07/21/the-lost-battalion-of-world-war-i/ ; https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smithsonian-institution/cher-ami-the-iconic-world-war-i-carrier-pigeon-makes-his-debut-at-carnegie-hall-180983212/ ; https://dashboard.si.edu/object/cher-ami:nmah_425415
- **The Alphabet Made in Secret** (Hangul, 1443 to 1446): King Sejong invents a new script for ordinary people while scholars object (Choe Manri's petition, 1444). Fits: single clear inventor, opposition, a payoff you can see (every letter shaped like the mouth). Check first: how secret it was and who helped is debated (Korean sources disagree); keep to what both sides agree on. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ch%27oe_Malli ; https://www.korean.go.kr/eng_hangeul/setting/002.html ; https://languagelog.ldc.upenn.edu/nll/?p=69269
- (All five above are also meant to be retold natively in Korean; the Myeongnyang and Hangul stories are Korean history and fit 역사 한 방울 best.)
